## Concept observations

`braingraphconcept.webp` shows two explanatory brain-network diagrams. The left separates colored modules with labeled nodes and edges. The right shows integrated nodes with a labeled connector hub. Transferable rule: show bounded modules, named nodes, typed edges, and integration/segregation as explicit data relationships. Do not copy labels or imply that visual proximity proves causality.

## Style observations

`braingraphstyle1.jpg` uses a dark technical HUD composition, a large luminous brain, cyan/blue analytical surfaces, framed panels, heat-map side views, calibrated scales, and dense diagnostic chrome. Transferable rules: dark instrument canvas, cyan signal, restrained secondary heat colors, framed analytical regions, and a clear primary brain surface.

`braingraphstyle2.jpg` uses a dark close-up neural field with cyan, orange, and violet luminous nodes, organic depth, and fine connecting filaments. Transferable rules: sparse multi-color semantic signals, dark negative space, controlled glow, and an organic network texture. Avoid copying random/unbounded particles into evidence views.

## General idea observations

- `braingraphidea1.webp` shows a bright white/cyan brain made from dense fine connections on a deep blue background. Useful for a contained brain activity surface and constellation-like signal texture.
- `braingraphidea2.webp` places a holographic brain in a futuristic room with orbiting cyan/amber paths. Useful for a command-deck atmosphere, but room-scale decoration is not needed in the app shell.
- `braingraphidea3(without person).webp` shows a floating cyan brain with holographic interfaces and a dark operator context. Apply the filename exclusion: no person. Useful for a focused brain hero or activity panel.
- `braingraphidea4(only brain and data, no room or table, etc).webp` shows a luminous brain with data overlays in a bright technical setting. Apply the filename exclusion: no room or table. Useful for brain-plus-data composition, but stock watermarks and unknown license exclude direct asset reuse.
- `braingraphidea5.webp` shows a small brain on a large technical display surrounded by cyan/orange dashboards. Useful for pipeline and observatory framing, not as a literal background.

## Design implications

1. Make the brain materially larger than the current concept mockups. It is the primary activity surface, not a small decorative card.
2. Contain actual activity inside the brain silhouette or an explicitly bounded brain frame. Draw only service-projected facts such as active count, named stages, selected relationship nodes, waiting, or unknown.
3. Reuse a restrained cyan base with sparse amber/violet/coral semantic accents. Keep labels and status words adjacent to any signal color.
4. Keep implementation concept semantics from `braingraphconcept.webp`: modules, nodes, edges, and connector hubs need explicit meaning and source identity.
5. Extend the activity visual language to Atlas relationships only when the data projection supplies bounded actual nodes and typed edges. Keep the DOM list/table as the accessible primary path.
6. Use vector/CSS or other local reviewed assets. Do not copy stock/watermarked images, external URLs, or unknown-license graphics into production.
7. Avoid decorative room/table/person imagery in the app surface. The filename guidance explicitly excludes those contexts for idea 3 and idea 4.
8. Motion remains optional, evidence-bound, reduced-motion safe, and never a substitute for actual progress or coverage.
